MY BEST EVERY DAY
The Practice Gym for the Mind
Core Vision
My Best Every Day is a personal gym for developing the inner strength to meet life consciously.
Just as a physical gym gives you different exercises depending on the muscles you want to strengthen, the Mind Gym gives you different practices depending on what is happening in your life and what your mind is getting stuck on.
You don't come to the gym because something is wrong with you.
You come because:
Your mind, attention, awareness, emotional resilience and ability to choose can be strengthened through practice.
Some days you may simply want to explore and strengthen yourself.
Other days, something specific may be occupying your mind:
“My friend ignored me.”
“I'm worried about tomorrow.”
“My parents are arguing.”
“I keep getting angry at my brother.”
“I can't stop thinking about what happened.”
“I'm nervous about school.”
“I made a mistake and can't stop replaying it.”
“Someone said something about me and I can't let it go.”
“I'm worried about a meeting at work.”
“I keep thinking about what my boss said.”
“I'm having trouble letting go of a relationship.”
“I know what I should do, but I keep reacting the same way.”
Instead of giving the person a generic meditation or article, the app says:
“Bring it into the gym. Let's work with it.”

1. THE FUNDAMENTAL PRODUCT LOOP
The entire product should revolve around:
LIFE HAPPENS
↓
I NOTICE SOMETHING IS STUCK IN MY MIND
↓
I BRING IT TO THE GYM
↓
THE GYM UNDERSTANDS MY SITUATION
↓
A BESPOKE PRACTICE IS CREATED
↓
I PRACTISE
↓
I NOTICE WHAT HAPPENS INSIDE ME
↓
I TRY A DIFFERENT WAY OF RESPONDING
↓
I BUILD INNER STRENGTH
↓
I RETURN TO REAL LIFE
↓
I COME BACK TOMORROW AND PRACTISE AGAIN
This is the core product loop.
Not:
Read lesson → answer quiz → receive badge.
But:
Experience life → bring experience → practise → grow → return to life.

The physical gym metaphor should be deeply embedded in the product.
A physical gym has:

Your Mind Gym has:

The user isn't being “fixed.”

3. TWO GYMS
The product should have two distinct experiences:
🧒 KIDS GYM
For children approximately 5–10.
The experience should be:
visual
playful
character-led
story-based
interactive
simple
safe
experiential
Children might enter:
🌈 Feelings Room
Learn to recognise emotions.
🧠 Thought Room
Discover what the mind is saying.
🫀 Body Detective Room
Notice where feelings show up physically.
🌬️ Pause Room
Practise creating space before reacting.
🎭 Different Story Room
Explore how the same event can feel different depending on the story the mind creates.
🤝 Friendship Room
Practise difficult friendship situations.
😤 Anger Room
Practise what to do when anger takes over.
😰 Worry Room
Practise meeting worry without letting it run everything.
❤️ Kindness Room
Practise kind actions and words.
⭐ Reflection Room
Look back and discover what was learned.
The child should feel:
“I am learning how my mind works.”

4. ADULT GYM
The Adult Gym should not feel like a larger version of the Kids Gym.
It should feel more sophisticated, calm and private.
The adult might enter and see:
Good morning. What would you like to work on today?
OPTION A — I HAVE SOMETHING ON MY MIND
“Tell us what's happening.”
The user types or speaks naturally:
“I keep getting angry whenever my partner questions my decisions.”
The system doesn't immediately give generic advice.
It first helps unpack:
What happened?
What did you feel?
What did your mind say?
What did you want to do?
What are you holding onto?
What are you afraid might happen?
Then:
“Let's build a practice around this.”

5. BESPOKE PRACTICE ROOM
This is potentially your killer feature.
The user describes their real-life situation.
The system dynamically creates a temporary or persistent Practice Room specifically for that situation.
For example:
User
“Every time my manager criticises my work, I feel defensive and spend the whole evening thinking about it.”
The app creates:
🧠 DEFENSIVENESS PRACTICE ROOM
What we're practising
Receiving criticism without immediately becoming defensive.
Your pattern
Event
Manager gives criticism.
↓
Thought
“They don't appreciate me.”
↓
Feeling
Defensive / angry.
↓
Urge
Explain → defend → replay conversation.
↓
Today's practice
Notice the moment before you react.
Then the app creates several exercises.

6. THE PRACTICE ROOM SHOULD FEEL ALIVE
Don't make the bespoke room a page of advice.
It should be an interactive training environment.
For example:
Exercise 1 — Replay
The situation is recreated.
“Your manager says:
 ‘I think this needs more work.’”
The user experiences the trigger.
Then:
What happens inside you?
They select:
😡 Anger
 😟 Fear
 😞 Hurt
 🛡️ Defensiveness
Then:
What does your mind say?
Possible thoughts appear.
“They think I'm incompetent.”
“I need to prove myself.”
“They're unfair.”
The user can add their own.

Exercise 2 — Become the Observer
The practice shifts.
“Notice the thought.”
The thought appears as a floating object.
“I am noticing the thought:
 ‘They think I'm incompetent.’”
The system doesn't tell them:
“That thought is wrong.”
Instead:
“Can you notice it without immediately believing it or acting on it?”
That's where the Tolle/Singer-inspired philosophy becomes experiential rather than theoretical.

7. THE CORE TRAINING ENGINE
Over time, the Practice Engine should learn to construct practices around:
EVENT
What happened?
THOUGHT
What story did the mind create?
FEELING
What emotion appeared?
BODY
What sensations appeared?
URGE
What did you want to do?
AWARENESS
Can you notice what's happening?
PAUSE
Can you create space?
CHOICE
What options do you have?
ACTION
What would you like to practise doing?
CONSEQUENCE
What happened?
LEARNING

This becomes the underlying data model of the entire application.

8. NOT EVERYONE NEEDS A BESPOKE ROOM
This is important.
You described two different types of users.
Type A — “Nothing specific is bothering me.”
The gym should say:
“Let's train something today.”
Generate a daily practice.
For example:
Today's Practice
🌬️ Practise pausing before reacting.
Tomorrow:
👀 Practise noticing thoughts.
Next day:
❤️ Practise seeing another person's perspective.
The experience is different each time.

Type B — “Something is stuck in my mind.”
The gym should say:
“Let's work on that.”
They describe the situation.
The system creates:
Your Personal Practice Room
And the user works on that specific situation.

9. THE PRACTICE SHOULD EVOLVE
This is where the product becomes much more interesting than a chatbot.
Suppose someone says:
“I can't stop thinking about an argument with my partner.”
Day 1:
NOTICE
What happened?
What am I feeling?
What is my mind saying?

Day 2:
OBSERVE
Can I notice the thought without immediately following it?

Day 3:
SEPARATE
What actually happened?
What story am I adding?

Day 4:
FEEL
Can I allow the feeling to be present without immediately trying to get rid of it?

Day 5:
PAUSE
What happens if I create space before responding?

Day 6:
PERSPECTIVE
What might the other person be experiencing?

Day 7:
CHOICE
What response would align with the person I want to be?

Day 8:
RELEASE
What am I still holding onto?

Day 9:
REAL LIFE
Practise responding differently in an actual situation.

Day 10:
INTEGRATE
“What have you discovered about yourself?”
This is a 10-Day Mind Strength Challenge.

10. USER-CONTROLLED PRACTICE
Don't make the AI permanently decide what the person needs.
The user should be able to say:
“This practice is helping.”
or
“I want to change this.”
or:
“I want to work on something else.”
They can:
SAVE PRACTICE
Save to My Gym
MODIFY
Change this practice
PAUSE
Come back later
COMPLETE
I feel ready to move on
CREATE CHALLENGE
Turn this into a 10-day challenge
This gives the user ownership.

11. THE AI PRACTICE COACH
This is where your chatbot fits.
But I would not position it simply as “a psychological expert.”
That creates unnecessary expectations and safety problems.
Position it as something like:
🧠 MY PRACTICE COACH
“Tell me what's on your mind. I'll help you explore it and create something you can practise.”
The coach should be able to:
ask clarifying questions
identify patterns
distinguish event/thought/feeling/urge
suggest practical exercises
create a practice room
create a multi-day challenge
modify practices
reflect on progress
suggest different perspectives
encourage awareness
help the user identify choices
But it should not diagnose.
And it shouldn't pretend:
“I know exactly what psychological condition you have.”
Instead:
“It sounds like this situation is bringing up a lot of fear.”
“Would you like to explore what your mind is telling you in that moment?”

12. TOLLE + SINGER AS THE INVISIBLE PHILOSOPHICAL ENGINE
This is a very good direction, provided you keep the distinction between philosophical inspiration and clinical/scientific authority.
Your internal knowledge layer can draw from the materials you provide, including the books you store in /docs.
But the product shouldn't become:
“Eckhart Tolle says…”
“Michael Singer says…”
on every screen.
Instead, the ideas become the design philosophy.
Tolle-inspired
Notice the present moment.
Singer-inspired
Notice the inner voice rather than automatically becoming it.
Combined child-friendly experience
“Your mind is talking.”
“You can hear it.”
“You can notice it.”
“You don't have to follow every thought.”
“There is a space between what happens and what you choose.”
That is powerful.
For the adult experience, you can allow deeper explanations and references to source material.
And because the books are copyrighted, instruct Claude to use provided materials as reference material, not to reproduce large passages or create a substitute copy of the books.

13. THE MOST IMPORTANT PRODUCT CONCEPT: “WHAT'S STUCK?”
I'd actually make this the front door of the adult experience.
When someone opens the gym:
What are you training today?
🔥 Something is bothering me

🌱 I want to grow

14. THE GYM SHOULD REMEMBER THE PERSON
Over time, the application builds a Personal Practice Profile.
Not:
“John is an angry person.”
Instead:
“John has practised noticing anger in situations involving criticism.”
That's an enormous difference.
The system might eventually show:
Your Practice Journey
Things you've practised
🔥 Reacting to criticism
 ❤️ Relationship expectations
 😰 Worry about work
 🧠 Overthinking
 🤝 Difficult conversations
Strengths you're building
👀 Awareness

The user begins seeing:
“I'm actually getting stronger.”

15. PROGRESS SHOULD MEASURE PRACTICE, NOT MORALITY
This is another critical principle.
Don't say:
You are 87% emotionally healthy.
That's both misleading and potentially harmful.
Instead:
Mind Strength
Awareness ⭐⭐⭐⭐

“Areas you've been practising.”

18. YOUR BIGGEST DIFFERENTIATOR
There are many meditation apps.
There are many journaling apps.
There are many therapy/chat apps.
There are many children's emotional-learning apps.
Your differentiation could be:
“Don't just tell me what to do. Let me practise the exact situation I'm struggling with.”
That is a much stronger proposition.
For example:
Headspace: “Here's a meditation.”
Journal: “Write about your problem.”
Chatbot: “Here's some advice.”
Your product:
“Show me what's happening. I'll turn that real-life situation into a practice environment where you can train how you meet it.”
That's a genuinely interesting product concept.

19. I WOULD CHANGE YOUR ORIGINAL PROJECT DOCUMENT
Your previous prompt should now not be called primarily an “emotional-awareness app.”
I'd make the top of your Product Vision say:
MY BEST EVERY DAY

THE PERSONAL PRACTICE GYM FOR THE MIND

PRODUCT NORTH STAR

My Best Every Day is a personalised practice gym for children
and adults.

Life continually gives us experiences that challenge us.

Sometimes nothing specific is bothering us and we simply want
to strengthen ourselves.

Sometimes one particular situation gets stuck in our mind.

The product meets both needs.

When nothing specific is present, the gym provides varied,
age-appropriate practices that build awareness, presence,
emotional resilience, perspective, conscious choice and
self-understanding.

When something specific is stuck in the user's mind, the user
can bring that real-life situation into the gym.

The system helps them understand:

EVENT
→ THOUGHT
→ FEELING
→ BODY
→ URGE
→ PAUSE
→ CHOICE
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ LEARNING

It then creates a personalised Practice Room around that
specific situation.

The Practice Room is not a page of advice.

It is an interactive training environment.

The user can replay the situation, notice thoughts and feelings,
experiment with different perspectives, practise creating space
before reacting, explore possible choices, and reflect on what
they discover.

If the situation requires repeated practice, the user can save
the Practice Room and turn it into a personalised multi-day
challenge, such as a 10-Day Practice.

The practice can evolve as the user learns.

The objective is not to "fix" the person.

The objective is to help the person build the capacity to meet
life with greater awareness and conscious choice.

The gym grows with the person.

Every practice is another repetition.

Every repetition can build strength.

Every day is another opportunity to practise.
